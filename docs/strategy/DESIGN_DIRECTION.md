## Chosen approach: The Operator’s Fieldbook

### Design Movement
Contemporary **editorial information design** with cues from operational field manuals and industrial wayfinding. The interface should resemble an informed team’s working dossier rather than a generic SaaS dashboard.

### Core Principles
1. **Evidence before decoration:** Every visual element either improves orientation, establishes hierarchy, or communicates a decision.
2. **Actionable at a glance:** Key decisions, risks, and next actions should be readable as an operator scans vertically through the report.
3. **Human accountability:** Automation is framed as controlled reach extension, with explicit escalation points and governance gates.
4. **Measured contrast:** Dense reasoning is balanced with generous whitespace, structured cards, and selected high-signal visual moments.

### Color Philosophy
The base is parchment-white and soft mineral-gray, conveying clarity and good judgment. Deep ink-navy holds analytical content, while **Signal Vermilion** (#E6533C) is the ownable, unmistakable brand color used only to designate an active decision, risk, or important control. A restrained bottle-green supports positive pathways and approved readiness states, grounding the report in the beverage-alcohol context without relying on literal imagery.

### Layout Paradigm
Use an **asymmetric fieldbook** structure: a narrow persistent left rail supplies orientation and progress, while the main document moves through full-bleed sections, offset decision cards, and “margin-note” annotations. Do not center every section into identical rounded-card grids. Let individual sections have visibly different rhythms—timeline bands, branching decision maps, and a financial-control dashboard.

### Signature Elements
1. A vertical “route line” in the navigation rail that marks the active section and the roadmap sequence.
2. Red index tabs and clipped-corner callouts for non-negotiable decisions, risks, and controls.
3. Fine ruled lines and micro-label metadata that evoke a field report without mimicking paper skeuomorphism.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions are deliberate and utility-first. Charts reveal exact context on hover, report filters change what a leader needs to focus on, and expandable controls disclose supporting evidence without overwhelming the primary narrative. Buttons should read like commands: “Review pilot gate,” “Compare scenarios,” and “Export briefing.”

### Animation
Content may enter with brief, staggered upward transitions (180–260ms) that suggest a report being assembled. The active route line should glide to the current section on navigation. Chart transitions are reserved for user-initiated filtering; use transform and opacity only, respect reduced-motion preferences, and avoid looping ambient effects.

### Typography System
Use **Instrument Serif** for decisive editorial headlines and **Manrope** for body copy, labels, tables, and analytical controls. Headlines use strong size contrast and modest tracking; labels appear in uppercase with careful letter spacing. No Inter. The prose must read comfortably at report length, while data labels remain compact and precise.

### Brand Essence
**A practical AI implementation fieldbook for beverage-alcohol operators who need better coverage, clearer controls, and a credible path from pilot to proof.**

**Personality:** Grounded, disciplined, forward-looking.

### Brand Voice
Headlines state a decision or create productive tension; CTAs sound like an operational next step, not a marketing funnel. Microcopy names constraints directly rather than disguising them.

Example: “Start with the missed reorder—not a CRM replacement.”

Example: “Automation earns trust when the human handoff is visible.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Create a bold, text-free **route-and-signal mark**: a simplified vermilion path that turns through a dark navy reference point, resembling both a distribution route and a deliberate decision gate. The symbol should remain recognizable at favicon scale, use a transparent background, and avoid letters or generic AI motifs.

### Signature Brand Color
**Signal Vermilion — #E6533C**

## Style Decisions

- **Harborline Command extension:** The live sales demo translates the Operator’s Fieldbook into a maritime operations room for account coverage: a deep-navy command shell, warm evidence surfaces, route traces, coverage arcs, signal grids, and accountable human handoffs.
- **Command material hierarchy:** Dark navy anchors the command environment; warm off-white holds evidence and account context; Signal Vermilion is reserved for risk, active action, and operator intervention; sea-green signals eligibility, control, coverage health, and approved state.
- **Operational typography:** Executive headlines use forceful scale and tight spacing; uppercase mono labels classify technical context; small evidence details remain precise and secondary. The result should read as an operating tool rather than a generic SaaS dashboard.
- **Controlled signal routing:** Every key product state uses visible routing lines, checkpoints, node markers, or evidence stamps to show how a communication moves through governed human control rather than disappearing into an opaque automation layer.
- **Strict color semantics:** Signal Vermilion denotes the human decision, intervention, risk, or required action; sea-green denotes a verified, approved, safe, or system-passed state. Evidence views should bias toward sea-green rather than use vermilion as decorative emphasis.
