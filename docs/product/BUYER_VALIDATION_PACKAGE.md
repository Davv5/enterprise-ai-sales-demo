# Harborline Buyer Validation Package

## What this package is for

This package helps the sales partner test whether **Harborline Communication Ops** solves a real workflow worth paying for. It does not ask a buyer whether they “like AI.” It asks them to describe what they do today when a relevant account needs a follow-up, where that process breaks, and whether Harborline’s controlled workflow would make that work easier, safer, or more accountable.

> **One sentence to use:** “We are testing a workflow that helps your team run routine retailer communication more consistently, while keeping a named rep in control of relationship-sensitive decisions.”

## The validation target

Harborline’s first product hypothesis is that a distributor can improve account coverage by using a governed workflow for a routine signal such as “no reorder in X days.” The product identifies the moment, checks account context and owner, prepares an approved availability message, uses the right level of human approval, routes non-routine replies to the rep, and keeps a visible record.

The interview round should not attempt to validate every future product idea. It should determine whether this **one workflow** is useful enough to justify a focused pilot.

## Who to speak with

| Interview group | Target count | What they can validate | Suggested contact role |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| **Distributor sales leaders** | 2 | Business pain, current process, sponsorship, and success criteria. | VP/Director of Sales, General Manager, Commercial Lead. |
| **Field representatives** | 3 | Daily usability, relationship risk, approval preference, and handoff behavior. | Territory Manager, Account Executive, Route/Field Sales Rep. |
| **Operations or compliance stakeholders** | 1 | Data source, ownership, communication controls, evidence expectations, and implementation constraints. | Sales Operations, IT, Compliance, Revenue Operations. |
| **Retailer buyers** | 2 | Whether the proposed communication is relevant, welcome, and appropriately timed. | Beverage Buyer, Store Owner, Bar/Restaurant Manager. |

The recommended first round is **eight conversations**. If access is limited, begin with two leaders, two representatives, one operations stakeholder, and one retailer buyer; record the gaps in the evidence log rather than silently assuming an answer.

## Questions the research must answer

| Hypothesis | Evidence that supports it | Evidence that weakens it | Product consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **H1: Routine follow-ups are missed or inconsistent.** | People describe no-reorder, allocation, or availability moments that depend on memory, email, text, spreadsheets, or delayed tasks. | The current system already handles the workflow reliably with clear ownership and follow-up. | If weak, do not sell Harborline as a replacement; find a different workflow. |
| **H2: A rep-approved message is acceptable.** | Representatives say a prepared message with a clear approval step would save time without harming the relationship. | Reps insist every message must be authored from scratch or any prefill is unacceptable. | Begin with Draft for Rep rather than Rep-Approved Send. |
| **H3: Exceptions need a named person.** | Users identify pricing, allocation, complaints, special terms, or account disputes as cases requiring human judgment. | The workflow is truly simple and never creates meaningful exceptions. | Keep human handoff as a core safety feature unless evidence says it adds no value. |
| **H4: Retailer outreach has a trust boundary.** | Buyers want brief, relevant, relationship-aware messages tied to a real need. | Buyers view automated distributor outreach as spam or only want ordering-platform contact. | Limit the channel, frequency, or user audience; consider buyer-initiated workflows instead. |
| **H5: Harborline can sit beside existing systems.** | Buyers name a CRM/ERP/order system that holds data but does not cleanly govern this communication path. | The incumbent already creates and controls the full workflow well enough. | Position Harborline as an integration extension only where a real gap exists. |

## The 25-minute conversation

| Timing | What the partner does | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0–3 minutes** | Introduce the problem, clarify that the demo is fictional, and ask permission to learn rather than sell. | Prevents leading the participant into approving your idea. |
| **3–10 minutes** | Ask them to walk through a recent real example of a missed reorder, product availability moment, allocation, or follow-up. | A real story exposes systems, people, workarounds, and failure points. |
| **10–15 minutes** | Show the Harborline Workflow Center and Reorder Reminder. Do not claim integration or live delivery. | Lets them react to a specific workflow instead of an abstract concept. |
| **15–20 minutes** | Show the human exception and audit state. Ask what they would change. | Tests the approval boundary and whether visible evidence helps or creates burden. |
| **20–25 minutes** | Ask for a pilot decision signal: one workflow, one team, one owner, one success measure. | Converts a conversation into a disciplined next step. |

## Open with these exact words

> “I am not asking whether you want more AI. I am trying to understand how your team handles a routine account moment today—such as a retailer who has not reordered—and whether a controlled workflow would help you stay consistent without taking judgment away from the rep.”

## Core discovery prompts

| Area | Prompt | Listen for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recent reality** | “Tell me about the last time an account should have reordered or needed a follow-up. What happened?” | Trigger, timing, owner, tool stack, and breakdown. |
| **Current system** | “Where does your team see the signal today, and what happens next?” | CRM/ERP/order portal, spreadsheet, inbox, task list, or memory. |
| **Human control** | “Which parts could a system prepare, and which parts must always stay with the rep?” | Draft, approval, policy boundaries, and exception categories. |
| **Retailer experience** | “How do your buyers prefer to hear from you when there is a legitimate account moment?” | Channel, frequency, context, trust, and opt-in constraints. |
| **Exception** | “What types of replies should never receive an automated answer?” | Price, terms, quantity, credit, dispute, complaint, allocation, or relationship issues. |
| **Proof of value** | “If this worked, what would improve first?” | Fewer missed follow-ups, time saved, faster response, better visibility, or more consistent ownership. |
| **Pilot** | “Would you test this with one workflow and a small account segment? What would make that safe enough to try?” | Sponsor, team, data, workflow boundary, and success metric. |

## How to show the demo

Show only the sequence below unless the participant asks for more. The goal is feedback, not a feature tour.

1. Open **Workflow Center** and explain that the system surfaces a controlled communication workflow, not a generic dashboard.
2. Open **Reorder Reminder** and show the trigger, eligible account, representative, preferred channel, and Rep-Approved mode.
3. Show the approved availability-check template and the explicit exclusion of price, promotion, and commercial terms.
4. Simulate a send, then show the non-routine reply and named human assignment.
5. Open **Audit & Policy** and ask whether the evidence trail is useful, sufficient, or excessive.

## Do not lead the witness

Avoid: “Would this save you time?”  
Ask: “What, if anything, would this change in your current process?”

Avoid: “You would want automated messages, right?”  
Ask: “Which level of control would you accept: draft only, rep approval, or a policy-approved routine message? Why?”

Avoid: “Our audit trail is important.”  
Ask: “What information would your manager need to see after this happened?”

## What counts as a strong signal

A strong signal is a specific example, not praise. For example: “We lose visibility after the account stops ordering; the rep only notices when they happen to look at a report,” or “I would allow a prepared availability email, but no price or allocation language without my review.” Capture the participant’s actual words and the context in the evidence log.
