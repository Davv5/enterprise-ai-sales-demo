# AI Operations Lead — Daily Operating Brief Specification

**Status:** Approved product direction for the next premium-demo specification.  
**Current product name:** TBD. Do not rename implementation code until a brand direction has been selected and screened.  
**Demo truth:** All account data, activity, AI observations, messages, controls, and outcomes remain fictional and simulated. No live integration, model inference, customer delivery, commercial decision, or legal/policy approval is represented as real.

## 1. Product move

The product is not only a workflow guidance interface. Its differentiated promise is an **AI Operations Lead**: an intelligent operating layer that begins the day already aware of approved account moments, organizes what matters, prepares safe next steps, and keeps people accountable for judgment.

> **Core promise:** The AI Operations Lead runs the process so your people can own the relationships and decisions that matter.

This is not an autonomous salesperson, an unbounded “agent,” an ERP replacement, or a generic chat application. It is a governed operating system that turns trusted input signals into a visible, ordered, human-owned operating day.

## 2. The role it plays

The AI Operations Lead has a deliberately narrow, powerful role: it **observes approved signals, creates order, prepares bounded work, explains what it found, routes non-routine cases, and preserves operational memory**.

| AI Operations Lead may do | Human must do |
| --- | --- |
| Detect or receive an approved reorder, allocation, program-notice, or reply event. | Own the relationship and decide what is commercially appropriate. |
| Organize account moments by operating state and named owner. | Approve, edit, reject, assign, or resolve sensitive work. |
| Prepare a message using an approved template and approved facts. | Decide price, terms, quantity, allocation, eligibility exceptions, or special treatment. |
| Explain the input signal, rule, control, and next permitted action. | Authorize any communication posture that requires human approval. |
| Identify that a reply needs human judgment and route its context. | Answer the customer, negotiate, make commitments, or interpret policy. |
| Preserve the evidence trail and team operating memory. | Remain accountable for the business decision recorded. |

**Language discipline:** Say that the AI Operations Lead *organizes, prepares, explains, and routes*. Do not say it *decides, negotiates, promises, approves, or replaces*. In the fictional demo, “AI” is a product concept embodied through visible, rule-backed reasoning; the current codebase does not yet make a live model call.

## 3. The first seat: Sales Manager / Operations Lead

The first person in the stronger demo is a fictional **Sales Manager / Operations Lead**. This is the right opening seat because it makes the business impact visible: the prospect sees a team operating day, not a single outreach action.

The manager asks four questions at the start of the day:

1. **Where could account coverage break down today?**
2. **What work is safely prepared versus waiting for a person?**
3. **Who owns the next decision?**
4. **Which customer conversation must not be handled by automation?**

The AI Operations Lead answers those questions with visible evidence, named owners, and bounded actions. The rep, allocation specialist, and sales-operations owner become the second act of the story when the buyer opens an individual moment.

## 4. New first screen: Daily Operating Brief

The **Daily Operating Brief** replaces Workflow Center as the default demo entry point. It is not a conventional dashboard and must not become a wall of charts, vanity metrics, or feature widgets. It is a calm, premium morning briefing that makes the operation understandable in one minute.

### 4.1 Opening statement

Use a concise, role-shaped briefing rather than generic welcome copy. Example fictional language:

> **Good morning, Renee.**
> Your team has six account moments to protect today. Two are ready for a human decision, one needs policy review, and one customer question needs a person now.

The brief must visually distinguish **fictional demo state** from a live client claim. It should include a persistent `SANDBOX / SIMULATED OPERATING DAY` disclosure.

### 4.2 Information hierarchy

| Zone | What appears | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Briefing header** | Date/time label, role, named fictional owner, “AI Operations Lead” statement, simulation disclosure. | Establishes the buyer’s seat and the product’s intelligence promise. |
| **Today’s operating posture** | A small, legible set of fictional state totals: **prepared**, **needs human decision**, **policy review**, **customer exception**. | Shows control and operating consequence without using fabricated ROI. |
| **Priority account moments** | Three to five ordered narrative cards. Each shows the account moment, why it surfaced, current control state, named owner, and next permitted action. | Makes the AI feel like it is leading the day, not sending messages. |
| **Decision queue** | A compact list of items only a named person can unblock. | Makes human ownership a feature—not a fallback. |
| **Operating memory** | A short, chronological “what changed since yesterday” strip. | Shows continuity across signals, actions, replies, and decisions. |
| **Evidence access** | Every moment can open its trace: source signal, rules, exclusions, owner, and outcome. | Ensures the product feels inspectable rather than magical. |

### 4.3 Fictional day scenario

Use the existing three workflow scenarios but make them part of one coherent fictional operating day. The counts below are demo composition, not customer statistics or outcome claims.

| Priority | Moment | State | AI Operations Lead briefing | Human owner / next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Juniper Bottle House has not reordered Solara for 38 days. | **Needs rep decision** | “The cadence signal, owner, contact, and permitted availability-check draft are ready.” | Renee reviews or records the next action. |
| **2** | Fleetwood is one of three qualified accounts for a fictional Nila Reserve Gin allocation. | **Draft ready** | “Interest-check context is prepared; quantity and price are intentionally excluded.” | Marcus decides whether to send or handle the opportunity. |
| **3** | A fictional Solara seasonal program notice has a locked twelve-account audience. | **Policy review** | “Terms reference and audience rule are present; distribution remains blocked pending review.” | Alicia verifies the gate. |
| **4** | Lina Cho asks about a prior price. | **Customer needs a person** | “This is a commercial judgment question. No response has been generated.” | Renee owns the response. |

These four moments make the system feel broad enough to run an operating day while retaining a coherent, controlled product boundary.

## 5. The six-minute seller experience

| Time | Seller action | What the prospect should feel | What the seller says |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0:00–0:45** | Open the Daily Operating Brief. | “I am in the seat of the person running the team today.” | “This is the AI Operations Lead’s morning brief. It has organized the account moments that need attention and separated work that is ready from work that still needs a person.” |
| **0:45–1:30** | Point to the four operating states and decision queue. | “It shows control, not noise.” | “It does not pretend every account needs automation. It shows what is prepared, what is blocked, and which decisions only a named person can make.” |
| **1:30–3:00** | Open Juniper Reorder. | “The AI gives my rep the right context, not a generic task.” | “For Juniper, it found the cadence signal and prepared an availability check. Renee decides whether it goes out. Price is excluded.” |
| **3:00–4:15** | Return to brief; open Fleetwood Allocation. | “The system understands that scarcity needs judgment.” | “Here the system organized an interest check but cannot promise quantity or terms. Marcus owns that decision.” |
| **4:15–5:15** | Open policy review and customer exception. | “It knows exactly where automation stops.” | “The notice cannot progress until the review is recorded. And when a buyer asks for special pricing, the system routes the conversation—without trying to negotiate.” |
| **5:15–6:00** | Open Operating Memory / evidence trace. | “Tomorrow, I can understand what happened without chasing messages.” | “This is how the team keeps operating memory: the signal, the control, the person, the action, and the next decision remain connected.” |

## 6. Interaction principles

1. **Brief before browse.** The buyer should receive a coherent operating interpretation before being asked to navigate.
2. **Why is always one click away.** Every priority card must expose the signal, rule, owner, allowed action, and excluded terms.
3. **Human decisions look consequential.** Approval, assignment, and policy review should visibly move the operating day, not merely change a badge.
4. **Exceptions elevate, not interrupt.** A customer question that needs judgment becomes a clearly owned priority, never a system failure.
5. **No intelligence theater.** Do not add a generic chat panel, fake confidence scores, invented predictions, or a black-box “AI decided” label.

## 7. Premium visual direction for this build

Retain the existing regulated-operations design language: navy authority, vermilion human intervention, mint verified control, route-and-record geometry, and evidence-stamp motifs. Expand it with a **morning briefing composition**:

- A large, restrained brief headline with a named operating role.
- A horizontal state ledger rather than a conventional KPI card grid.
- Narrative priority cards with clear cause → control → person → next action.
- A timeline-like Operating Memory strip that connects the day without looking like a chart.
- Low-motion, purposeful transitions when a human decision changes a priority state.

Avoid purple gradients, generic dashboard cards, rounded-card repetition, predictive-growth graphs, and centered “AI copilot” chat layouts.

## 8. First implementation slice

The first code slice is **Daily Operating Brief + role-shaped navigation**, built on top of the existing three workflow demo. It must be a focused composition change, not a full product rewrite.

### In scope

- Make the Daily Operating Brief the default home screen.
- Reuse the Reorder, Allocation, Promo, Replies & Exceptions, Accounts, and Audit views as drill-down destinations.
- Add the fictional operating posture ledger, priority account-moment stack, compact decision queue, and Operating Memory preview.
- Add a distinct AI Operations Lead briefing module that explains **why** each moment appeared using existing fictional rules and states.
- Ensure state changes in a workflow visibly return to and update the Daily Operating Brief.
- Preserve direct pitch-recovery URLs and Reset demo.
- Update the seller runbook and product brief only after the UI behavior is implemented and verified.

### Explicit non-goals

- No live LLM, model call, external API, database, authentication, integration, background job, real message delivery, price calculation, allocation decision, policy determination, or legal-compliance claim.
- No generic chat interface, predictive revenue forecast, real ROI claim, full CRM, ERP, catalog, marketplace, or bulk campaign tool.
- No permanent product rename or visual identity rewrite until brand selection is decided.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

The Daily Operating Brief implementation is ready for review only when all of the following are true:

1. In the first 30 seconds, a prospect can identify the fictional role, today’s operating posture, the next human decisions, and that the product is simulated.
2. Each priority moment visibly states: **why it surfaced**, **who owns it**, **what is safely prepared**, **what remains prohibited**, and **the next permitted action**.
3. The existing Reorder, Allocation, and Policy Notice flows remain complete and truthful through trigger, preparation, simulated action, exception, assignment, and audit.
4. A Rep, Marcus, or Alicia action updates the brief’s operating state without implying a real send, real policy approval, or commercial outcome.
5. The customer exception looks like a human-priority event on the brief and opens the existing named-owner handoff.
6. Every current direct pitch-recovery URL still works; new direct states are documented if added.
7. Desktop and mobile retain navigation, Reset demo, and a persistent fictional/simulated disclosure.
8. Typecheck, production build, repository integrity checks, and full seller click paths pass.
