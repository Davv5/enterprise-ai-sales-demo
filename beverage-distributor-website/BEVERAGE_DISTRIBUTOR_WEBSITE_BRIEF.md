# Beverage Distributor Website Brief

## Decision

The existing Communication Ops website is an effective **category-level product story**, but its language deliberately stays broad. The beverage version should not replace it. It should make the same controlled operating model feel familiar to distributor sales and operations leaders by grounding it in the account moments, channels, and relationship handoffs that shape their day.

## Audience Insight

Beverage-alcohol distributors do substantially more than move inventory: they support supplier brand strategy and route-to-market execution, work with licensed on- and off-premise accounts, and help retailers with delivery, merchandising, category expertise, menu development, and training.[1] Their teams operate between supplier expectations, account relationships, field activity, program execution, and a regulated three-tier environment.[2] [3]

> The website should lead with **the account moment a rep recognizes**, then show the disciplined handoff that protects the relationship. It should not lead with a generic “AI platform” abstraction.

| Broad baseline language | Beverage-specific translation |
| --- | --- |
| Account signal | A route, shelf, cooler, menu, allocation, or account-cadence moment |
| Prepared work | A rep-ready follow-up with the buyer, account history, and program context attached |
| Human decision | A named manager or account owner holding pricing, allocation, terms, or exception judgment |
| Operating memory | The field-to-account record: what surfaced, which account was involved, who followed up, and what still needs judgment |

## Recommended Positioning

**Communication Ops keeps the account moments your beverage team sees every day from getting lost between the route, the buyer, and the back office.**

The most credible frame is neither “AI sells beverage” nor “automated account messaging.” It is an **account-coverage and controlled-handoff layer** for teams whose real advantage is the trust built by a rep, buyer, and supplier relationship.

The story should make four buyer truths visible:

1. **The field knows first.** A route, buyer conversation, display check, menu opportunity, or change in account cadence can be the earliest useful signal.
2. **Not every account is the same.** On-premise and off-premise work operate differently, while chain programs, independents, seasonal calendars, and supplier priorities create distinct paths.[1] [4]
3. **A message is not a decision.** The product prepares context and routes the work; it does not decide pricing, allocation, legal interpretation, terms, or whether a customer communication is authorized.
4. **The relationship is the operating asset.** Beverage distribution works through local, repeated interaction across suppliers, distributors, and retailers; the product must reinforce, not erase, the accountable human owner.[1] [2]

## Copy Territory

### Recommended Hero

> **The account moment starts in the field. Keep it moving all the way to the buyer.**

> From a cooler reset to a chain-program question, Communication Ops gives every signal the right context, a named owner, and a controlled next step—before the account thread goes cold.

### Alternate Hero

> **Your reps know the account. Your operation should remember the moment.**

> Communication Ops connects the route, the account, and the next responsible action for beverage teams that win on trust and execution.

### CTA Direction

Use a specific invitation such as **“Walk through a distributor day”** or **“See the account handoff”** rather than generic “Explore the brief.”

## Website Scenario Design

The beverage version should use fictional examples without presenting them as real customers, live messages, or legal guidance.

| Scenario | Familiar account moment | Visible human boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Route rhythm check** | A core SKU or account order cadence changes after a field visit or route observation. | The rep decides the follow-up and owns the customer conversation. |
| **Seasonal display handoff** | A store display or supplier program needs a timely account check before the seasonal window closes. | The manager approves program terms and any commercial exception. |
| **Allocation conversation** | A limited item needs an interest check with an account that fits the agreed audience. | Allocation, quantity, price, and account eligibility remain human decisions. |
| **On-premise menu follow-up** | A bar, restaurant, hotel, or chain account has a menu, placement, or training-related next step. | The account owner decides what is appropriate for the buyer and local operating context. |

## Visual Direction

Retain the baseline’s disciplined editorial quality, but trade its generic documentation still lifes for **field-to-account cues**: a route sheet, a cooler-door reflection, a case label, a seller’s account notebook, a glassware/menu edge, or a printed display plan. These details should imply a working beverage account day without depicting alcohol consumption, real retail brands, or a legal/compliance seal.

## Deliberate Constraints

The site must not claim live integrations, delivery, legal or policy approval, promotional compliance, financial outcomes, or autonomous customer messaging. State-specific alcohol rules and authorized communication decisions remain outside the product’s automated role.
